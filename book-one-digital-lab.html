Phospholipid bilayer

Phospholipids arrange with water-attracting heads toward watery spaces and water-avoiding tails facing inward. This creates a flexible, selectively permeable boundary.

Why it mattersThe membrane separates environments while allowing regulated exchange. Its properties help the cell maintain a distinct internal state.

Nucleus

The nucleus houses most of the cell’s DNA. Nuclear pores regulate exchange between the nucleus and cytosol; gene expression uses DNA information to support cell activity.

Community connectionOrganelles specialise, but none works alone. The cell’s behaviour emerges from coordinated activity and exchange.

Cellular specialisation

Select a traffic station

Endoplasmic reticulum

The endoplasmic reticulum helps make and prepare some proteins and lipids. Cargo can be packaged into transport vesicles for delivery to other parts of the cell.

Why routes matterCorrect sorting helps a molecule reach the place where it can function. Routes include checkpoints, and many pathways run at once.

Animate a simplified route. Watch for budding, movement, docking and membrane fusion.

PLAY THE ROUTE
Secretory route: cargo is packaged into a vesicle, transported, then may dock and fuse with a target membrane.

Endosome sorting decision

Material arriving from the cell surface can have different fates.

Think it through: endosomes sort internalised material. Some receptors recycle to the surface; other cargo can be directed toward lysosomal degradation.

Ion storage inside membrane compartments

Intracellular membranes also help create compartments with distinct ion conditions.

At rest: membranes and transport proteins help maintain different conditions in a compartment and the cytosol. A regulated channel can change ion movement.
Clinical connection · Mucolipidosis type IV

04 · Blood: A Living Transport System

What is blood made of, and what does each part do?

Explore the cells, fluid and fragments that move materials, support defence and help limit blood loss.

Begin with a person · Mr Thomas

Mr Thomas’s blood is changing. Understanding the components can help explain what a test or proposed component might relate to—but it does not replace his questions or clinical assessment.

Click a blood component

Schematic only: the shapes are enlarged and the proportions are illustrative.

EXPLORE COMPONENTS
Select a component

Plasma

Plasma is the fluid portion of blood. It carries water, ions, nutrients, hormones, proteins, wastes and the blood cells and platelets that circulate within it.

How components connectBlood is a transport system: its components have different roles, but their work is linked through circulation and the wider body.

From blood-forming stem cell to circulating cell

Blood cells are produced and mature through regulated pathways, mainly in bone marrow in adults.

Blood-cell formation: haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells give rise to blood-cell lineages. Maturation and release are regulated; the pathway is more detailed than this overview.
04–05 · Connect and return

Blood components work together

Plasma carries cells and dissolved substances; red cells support oxygen transport; white cells contribute to immune defence; platelets help initiate haemostasis. A component choice in care depends on the person’s circumstances and clinical assessment.
